# Curcumin

The main polyphenol of turmeric (*Curcuma longa*), studied for anti-inflammatory and anticancer properties.

## Anticancer research: curcumin shows the best balance (2026 study)

Study: **Radzka, J., et al. (2026). "Modulation of NFκB Signaling by Natural Compounds in Sarcoma and Normal Muscle Models."** *Int. J. Mol. Sci.* 27(11), 5025 — DOI: [10.3390/ijms27115025](https://www.mdpi.com/1422-0067/27/11/5025). Wroclaw Medical University + University of Wroclaw.

Five natural compounds were compared — curcumin, berberine, biochanin A, cucurbitacin E, and CAPE (propolis) — on fibrosarcoma cells, healthy muscle cells, and greater wax moth larvae.

### Key findings

- **Energy metabolism:** all five compounds disrupted mitochondrial function. ATP fell ~83–92% in fibrosarcoma cells vs ~23–73% in healthy muscle cells — cancer cells appear more vulnerable to energy-metabolism disruption.
- **Senescence:** all five induced cellular senescence (cells stay alive but stop dividing) in fibrosarcoma cells. Curcumin was strongest: the senescent fraction rose from ~16.5% to **more than 75%**.
- **Selectivity:** curcumin and CAPE were the most selective against fibrosarcoma cells; curcumin combined strong anticancer activity with a relatively favorable safety profile — the **most balanced profile** of the five.
- **Whole-organism safety (wax moth larvae):** berberine and curcumin were best tolerated; CAPE and cucurbitacin E caused higher mortality; biochanin A was most toxic.
- **Mechanism focus:** modulation of the **NF-κB** pathway (inflammation, metabolism, survival, aging) — precise modulation, not total suppression.

### Limitations

In vitro cell lines plus an invertebrate model only. The authors explicitly state this does **not** mean curcumin can be used to treat sarcomas. Next steps: advanced in vivo models, mechanisms, optimal dosing and delivery.
