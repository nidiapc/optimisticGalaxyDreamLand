# Butyrate

A short-chain fatty acid produced when gut bacteria (e.g. Faecalibacterium prausnitzii, Roseburia) ferment dietary fiber; the primary energy source for colon cells.

## Gut barrier

Butyrate keeps tight junctions strong and maintains the mucus layer, preventing undigested food, bacteria, and toxins from leaking into the bloodstream and triggering systemic inflammation.

## Systemic benefits

- Crosses the blood-brain barrier (linked to lower Alzheimer's risk via Eubacterium / Eisenbergiella)
- Supports weight management and blood sugar control via hunger hormones
- Promotes apoptosis in damaged colon cells (lower colorectal cancer risk)

## How to increase it safely

- Eat a diverse range of fiber sources (fruits, vegetables, whole grains, beans, resistant starch from cooled potatoes/rice) plus fermented foods (kefir, sauerkraut, kimchi, grass-fed yogurt).
- Increase fiber gradually if gut health is compromised, to avoid endotoxin buildup.
- Ensure mitochondrial function is optimal first.
